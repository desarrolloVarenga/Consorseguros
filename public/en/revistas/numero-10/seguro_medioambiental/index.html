Environmental insurance and its significance for business risk management

Environmental insurance and its significance for business risk management

  • Alfonso Urquijo Fernández de Córdoba - Environmental Practice Leader Iberia Willis Towers Watson. Technical Area, ADECOSE
f X in

Introduction

Current and future legislation on environmental matters, the circular economy, and climate change, as well as Corporate Governance trends at both national and European levels, mean that organizations must be concerned with and address the proper financing of environmental risk. This can significantly influence an organization’s balance sheet and profit & loss account, as well as introduce new risks that are difficult to finance, such as reputational, image, and brand risks, or even share value for listed companies.

The new Code of Good Governance for Listed Companies of 2015 includes a specific principle (Principle 24) on corporate social responsibility (CSR):

“The company shall promote an appropriate corporate social responsibility policy as a non-delegable function of the board of directors, offering transparently sufficient information on its development, application, and results.”

This principle has as its predecessor Article 529 ter “Non-delegable Powers” of Royal Legislative Decree 1/2010 of July 2, which establishes that CSR policy is a non-delegable power of the board of directors (likewise introduced in Law 31/2014 of December 3, amending the Corporate Enterprises Act to improve corporate governance).

Another recommendation is that the board of directors endeavor to reconcile the company’s own corporate interest with, as appropriate, the legitimate interests of its employees, suppliers, customers, and other stakeholders that may be affected, as well as the impact of the company’s activities on the community as a whole and on the environment.

We believe we are at a moment in which society and business are advancing together transparently toward increasingly advanced risk communication and management policies, which will promote their development and growth hand in hand with both their shareholders and new investors.

Why do environmental risks threaten the financial health of companies?

The future tasks of companies will be increasingly conditioned by national and international regulatory frameworks that are growing stricter, as well as by a continuous focus on corporate governance obligations.

Additionally, the influence of regulatory authorities is gaining strength, involving “strict” liability and, in some cases, “unlimited liability.”

We must not forget that, although environmental risk is a risk of intensity rather than frequency, it can have a major impact regarding a company’s reputational and image risk.

On the other hand, in the future, we must pay attention to new risks arising from the circular economy and climate change, understood as emerging risks with potential high impact that, like the former, can affect the liability of directors and officers.

All of the above has driven a growing need to evaluate environmental risk management requirements in industry.

Mandatory Financial Guarantee under Law 26/2007, currently 11/2014

After seven years of standstill following the publication of Order ARM/1783/2011 of June 22, which established the priority order and schedule for approving the ministerial orders from which the constitution of the Mandatory Financial Guarantee would be required, the long-awaited Ministerial Order was published (Order APM/1040/2017 of October 23), setting the date from which the constitution of the Mandatory Financial Guarantee would be enforceable (October 31, 2018).

The mandatory date to carry out risk analyses and contract a financial guarantee for Priority 1 activities (if exemption scenarios could not be applied) was October 31, 2018; for Priority 2 activities, the date will be October 31, 2019, and for Priority 3 activities, we must await the publication of a new Ministerial Order establishing the mandatory schedule for that group’s activities.

It is important to remember that, although the approval of the Royal Decree amending the Regulations for partial development of the Environmental Liability Law significantly reduces the number of companies not subject to the obligation of establishing guarantees, “the non-mandatory nature of the financial guarantee does not exempt companies and business owners from liability.”

It is important to recall several priority points regarding environmental liability:

  • Law 11/2014 establishes liability for both natural and legal persons.
  • Strict and unlimited liability regarding damage caused for Appendix III activities, “and also for non-included activities when fraud, fault, or negligence is involved.”
  • The limit required for the financial guarantee does not limit the operator’s liability.
  • Article 13 of the Law establishes secondary liability for, among others, de facto and de jure managers and directors of legal entities whose conduct was decisive in the entity’s liability (hence the importance of having good Directors and Officers - D&O - liability coverage).
  • NGOs are interested parties in initiating proceedings, and the Prosecutor’s Office may appear.
  • The Criminal Code incorporates aggravated forms for offenses against natural resources and the environment.

Regardless of whether the Law establishes insurance as one of the valid financial guarantee instruments alongside bank guarantees or ad-hoc funds, it is important to note that the insurance sector belongs to the private domain and, therefore, can select whom to insure, what coverages to offer, and what price to charge for the risk borne. Therefore, to transfer environmental risks through this instrument, it will be important for companies to develop effective environmental policies that make the risk more attractive to insurance companies.

Market

The option of Environmental Liability insurance is perhaps the most practical and economic choice when transferring/financing the environmental risk of companies in the event of a potential contamination incident or environmental damage.

We are at a time when the insurance market has developed tailored services, programs, and products that are highly sophisticated and suitable for industry, although, to a large extent, these products have only been purchased by large corporations, listed companies, and a few responsible SMEs; however, many SMEs will not purchase it until it becomes mandatory for them.

Insurance companies have been making efforts to adapt their products to the new legislative framework. In this regard, it is important that the insurance market not only offer coverages based on the initiation of administrative proceedings under Law 26/2007 (currently 11/2014), but also expand that coverage commitment to the full set of potential environmental liabilities of their clients in a generic manner, to offer them a broad transfer of their risks.

The future of maintaining and growing portfolios in this branch will depend on service quality, management, and agility in claim payments and settlements, as well as being capable of producing international programs with high added value.

The current market situation is not as favorable as in past years. In the last year, we have seen certain market reservations regarding customer demand for premium reductions. Companies have compensated for the almost widespread refusal to reduce premiums with relative ease in improving coverage terms.

The hardening of the market is due to an increase in loss rates in two main sectors: the petrochemical sector and waste management. Added to this is the fact that major claims have occurred in the market, along with the prospect that there may also be others in other sectors in the future.

Nevertheless, the appearance of new insurance companies interested in these risks has somewhat cushioned the market hardening trend, and they continue to be quoted at competitive premiums.

Looking ahead to October 31, 2019, the insurance sector must prepare and adapt to the new demands and needs of the Spanish market, and we must be able to offer these solutions in a very agile and decisive manner so as not to jeopardize many years of work and our future credibility.

We believe that the enforceability of mandatory financial guarantees will have a positive effect on the degree of corporate responsibility and on the development of the insurance market regarding environmental matters, as the market must continue developing and adapting its policies to market needs.

Environmental coverages are of great relevance in the current context of judicialization of many claim processes, all while considering the upward trend toward the strictness of liability in many scenarios that were not so previously.

We believe there is a long way to go, that there are great growth opportunities in environmental liability product lines both nationally and internationally, as high demand exists in traditional activity sectors and projects with potential environmental impact (e.g., waste sector, energy, chemical, petrochemical, pharmaceutical, etc.), in newly created sectors, as well as in new projects arising from existing clients abroad generating high demand for international programs and services.

The main coverages in the market are:

  • Contractor coverage (operation of third-party risk situations).
  • Personal and/or material damages and financial losses to third parties occurring both inside and outside insured risk situations.
  • Cleanup costs outside insured risk situations.
  • Cleanup costs inside insured risk situations.
  • Environmental damage resulting from the transportation of the insured’s goods.
  • Business interruption due to environmental damage.
  • Electromagnetic fields and waves.
  • Environmental damage (includes primary, complementary, and compensatory repair).
  • Pollution, annoyance, and obstruction.
  • Prevention costs.
  • Mitigation and/or avoidance expenses.
  • Defense and bonds.

Regarding mandatory financial guarantee coverages, we find primary repair coverage up to the limit resulting from the risk analysis for this degree of repair, and coverage for prevention and avoidance expenses (10%).

Conclusions

Insurance now plays, more than ever, a crucial role in environmental risk management.

Considering the current insurance market, there are no reasons not to finance and transfer potential financial losses arising from environmental risks in any type of company.

Analyzing where we came from and where we are over these past 10 years, we can conclude that Spain is at the forefront in developing products and services aimed at improving the financing and management of environmental risks, far ahead of other countries in our environment.

The current market situation is not as favorable as in past years. In the last year, we have seen certain market reservations regarding customer demand for premium reductions. Companies have compensated for the almost widespread refusal to reduce premiums with relative ease in improving coverage terms. The hardening of the market is due to an increase in loss rates in two main sectors: the petrochemical sector and waste management. Added to this is the fact that major claims have occurred in the market, along with the prospect that there may also be others in other sectors in the future.
