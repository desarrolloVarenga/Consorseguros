The **Environmental Damage Compensation Fund (FCDM for its Spanish acronym)** was conceived as a necessary supplement to the financial guarantee implemented through an insurance contract to protect those operators with the most potential to pollute, in certain circumstances, with respect to their environmental liability obligations[cite: 11].

This article analyses the legal framework for environmental liability, the financial guarantee, and the FCDM based on the provisions of European and national legislation[cite: 11].

## The European Environmental Liability Directive
The FCDM arose from the financial guarantee required of operators of professional activities under **Directive 2004/35/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 21 April 2004, on environmental liability with regard to the prevention and remedying of environmental damage.**[cite: 11]

This regulation advocates incorporating an administrative regime of strict and unlimited environmental liability into the legal systems of Member States[cite: 11].

The core principle of the Directive is that the operator who causes environmental damage should be declared liable from a financial standpoint (recital 2 of the Directive)[cite: 11].

Recital 27 of the Directive refers to the need for Member States to encourage operators to use appropriate insurance or other forms of financial security to provide them with cover against enforceable financial obligations[cite: 11].

Article 14 of the Directive, titled “Financial Security,” includes the following aspects:

* It urges Member States to encourage operators and financial institutions to develop financial security instruments and markets to enable operators to cover their responsibilities under the Directive.
* It alludes to reasonable costs and conditions of insurance and other forms of financial security.
* It sets out the approach to financial security: (i) gradual application; (ii) with a ceiling for the financial guarantee, though not for liability; and (iii) the exclusion of low-risk activities[cite: 11].

On the other hand, Article 17 of the Directive determines the scope of temporal application, which exempts obligations arising from the norm when more than 30 years have passed since the event causing the damage took place[cite: 11].

Finally, it should be highlighted that the Directive establishes a special regime for the most potentially hazardous activities (those listed in Annex III) compared to other activities as regards the type of liability (strict or fault-based) and the type of obligation (preventive, avoidance, and remedial measures)[cite: 11].

There is therefore no reference at all in the Directive to setting up a fund, but it does define elements that will be decisive in creating one:

* Financial liability is involved.
* There must be financial instruments, with specific mention of insurance, capable of responding to such liability.
* Liability extends up to 30 years after the occurrence of the event responsible. This temporal reference is key to having a fund available for specific cases to supplement cover from insurance policies[cite: 11].

Under this Directive, approval was given for both **Law 26/2007, of 23 October, on Environmental Liability and the Regulations partly implementing Law 26/2007, of 23 October, on Environmental Liability** (Royal Decree 2090/2008, of 22 December)[cite: 11].

## Environmental Liability in Law 26/2007 and Royal Decree 2090/2008
For the purpose of examining the scope and functioning of the FCDM, the decisive aspects of environmental liability are described below as set out in the Law and the Regulations supplementing it[cite: 11].

1. **Scope of application** (Article 3 of the Law) and **Purpose of remedy** (Article 20 et seq. of the Regulations)[cite: 11].

    The scope of application of the legislation is as follows:

    * For activities in Appendix III of the Law: strict liability applies regarding measures to prevent, avoid, and remedy environmental damage.
    * For activities other than those listed in Appendix III, there are two options:
        * Fault-based liability (where intent, fault, or negligence is present) regarding measures to prevent, avoid, and remedy environmental damage.
        * Strict liability regarding measures to prevent and avoid environmental damage, without including remedial measures[cite: 11].

    Preventive measures are those taken in response to an event or omission that represents an immediate threat of environmental damage, aiming to prevent such damage as far as possible[cite: 11].

    Avoidance measures are those aimed at limiting further environmental damage once environmental damage has occurred[cite: 11].

    Remedial measures are intended to restore damaged natural resources or provide an equivalent alternative[cite: 11]. As established in Article 20 et seq. of the Regulations, remedial measures may be of three types: (i) primary, restoration of natural resources to their basic state; (ii) supplementary, when restoration is not possible or consumes too long a period of time or primary remedy is very costly; and (iii) compensatory for the provisional loss of natural resources during recovery[cite: 11].

2. **Temporal scope of environmental liability** (Article 4 of the Law)[cite: 11].

    Liability can be enforced on the operator up to 30 years after the day on which the event causing the environmental damage has completely come to an end[cite: 11].

## Financial guarantee in Law 26/2007 and Royal Decree 2090/2008
Having covered the basic aspects of environmental liability, we look now at the features of the financial guarantee as a prelude to analyzing the FCDM[cite: 11].

1. **Setting up a mandatory financial guarantee** (Article 24 of the Law) **and mandatory financial guarantee and notification to the competent authority** (Article 33 of the Regulations)[cite: 11].

    Operators in Appendix III of the Law, with the exemptions in Article 28 thereof, are obliged to take out a financial guarantee for the sum resulting from the environmental risk assessment carried out by the operator in line with the methodology defined in the legislation[cite: 11].

    The amount of the mandatory financial guarantee shall include the cost of primary remedy as well as the costs of damage prevention and avoidance[cite: 11]. This sum does not imply any ceiling on the operator's liability, which, as established by both the Directive and the Law, is unlimited[cite: 11]. For this reason, obliged operators may voluntarily secure their financial liability for a sum in excess of the amount resulting from their environmental risk assessment[cite: 11]. However, the guarantee voluntarily taken out shall not be subject to the requirements and special characteristics defined in the Law with respect to the mandatory financial guarantee[cite: 11].

    By the same token, other operators not obliged to set up financial security may voluntarily take out financial guarantees against environmental liability, which likewise shall not be subject to the requirements and special characteristics defined in the Law regarding the mandatory financial guarantee[cite: 11].

2. **Liability covered by the guarantee** (Article 25 of the Law)[cite: 11].

    The mandatory financial guarantee must be specifically and exclusively allocated to cover the environmental liability of operators on the terms indicated by the Law, separate from civil, administrative, or any other type of liability[cite: 11].

3. **Forms of guarantee** (Article 26 of the Law)[cite: 11].

    The possible financial instruments that can be used alternatively or as a supplement to cover the mandatory financial guarantee are:

    * Taking out an insurance policy: in this case, with a contribution to the FCDM and with the entitlements corresponding to such contribution.
    * Obtaining a bank guarantee.
    * Setting up a technical reserve[cite: 11].

4. **Guaranteed parties** (Article 27 of the Law)[cite: 11].

    The guaranteed parties are the operator and, additionally, subcontractors, collaborating professionals, and the owner of the facilities[cite: 11].

5. **Exemptions from setting up the mandatory financial guarantee** (Article 28 of the Law)[cite: 11].

    The following Appendix III operators are exempt from setting up the mandatory financial guarantee:

    * Those whose remedial costs are assessed at an amount below 300,000 euros.
    * Those whose remedial costs are between 300,000 and 2,000,000 euros, provided they prove adherence to the EMAS system or the UNE-EN ISO 14001 standard.
    * The use of phytosanitary products and biocides for agricultural, livestock, or forestry purposes.
    * Others determined by regulations[cite: 11].

6. **Costs covered** (Article 29 of the Law)[cite: 11].

    The mandatory financial guarantee must cover the costs of prevention, avoidance, and primary remedy[cite: 11].

7. **Quantitative limits of the guarantee** (Article 30 of the Law)[cite: 11].

    Regardless of the result of the environmental risk assessment carried out by the operator, the mandatory financial guarantee may not exceed 20,000,000 euros, but this does not exempt operators from covering the full costs of prevention, avoidance, and primary remedy[cite: 11].

8. **Guarantee period** (Article 31 of the Law) **and continuity of financial guarantee coverage** (Article 39 of the Regulations)[cite: 11].

    The guarantee must be set up from when its requirement becomes effective under the regulations in force until the cessation of activity[cite: 11].

    If taking out an insurance policy was chosen, the operator must justify: (i) the validity of the insurance policy; and (ii) the absence of misalignment among the various policies regarding coverage periods[cite: 11].

9. **Limitations on the temporal scope of the guarantee** (Article 32 of the Law)[cite: 11].

    The financial guarantee set up must respond to environmental damage that meets the following conditions: (i) it begins within the guarantee period; and (ii) the manifestation of the damage and the claim against the operator occur during the guarantee period or within the 3 years following its termination[cite: 11].

The basic time references for environmental liability and the mandatory financial guarantee are:

* The operator is liable for environmental damage up to 30 years after the polluting event ends.
* Operators obliged to take out a financial guarantee must maintain it from when it is required until they cease activity.
* The mandatory financial guarantee must respond to events starting in the guarantee period and manifested and claimed up to 3 years after policy expiration.
* If insurance policies are chosen, they must be designed so that there are no coverage gaps while activity continues or up to 3 years after activity ceases[cite: 11].

One can therefore observe a misalignment between the coverage under mandatory environmental liability insurance policies (effective for up to 3 years after activity ceases) and the temporal scope of environmental liability under the Law (extending up to 30 years after the polluting event ends, even if activity has ceased)[cite: 11].

In this context, the need arises to create a fund to handle environmental damage associated with ceased activities when the polluting event occurred during the operator's active period but manifested after the 3-year minimum period covered by insurance policies[cite: 11].

## FCDM in Law 26/2007 and Royal Decree 2090/2008
### The FCDM: legal nature
**Environmental Damage Compensation Fund of the Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros** (Article 33 of the Law and Article 44 of the Regulations)[cite: 11].

The functions performed by the Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros (CCS) can be of two types: insurance functions and other non-insurance public functions[cite: 11].

In the case of insurance functions, these must be provided for in its Legal Statute or approved by the Board of Directors by a two-thirds majority of its members[cite: 11].

In the case of non-insurance public functions, these must be attributed to it by legal norms under Article 16.e) of the Legal Statute of the CCS[cite: 11].

In this context, Law 26/2007 assigns to the CCS, as a non-insurance public function, the administration and management of the FCDM, with financial and accounting independence from the rest of its activities[cite: 11].
{{< /imagen-texto >}}

The CCS acts, pursuant to Article 33 of Law 26/2007, as a mere administrator and manager of the FCDM, whose resources are financially and accounting-wise independent from its other activities[cite: 11]. Thus, the CCS will not have to bear any payment related to environmental damage charged to the assets of any of its other activities[cite: 11].

Furthermore, delving deeper into its non-insurance nature, the Law regulates that benefits from the FCDM are ultimately limited to the total amount set up within it[cite: 11].

Under Order ARM/1783/2011 of 22 June and Order APM/1040/2017 of 23 October, the mandatory financial guarantee and, consequently, the FCDM came into effect progressively starting on 30 October 2018[cite: 11].

### The FCDM: contributions
**Environmental Damage Compensation Fund of the Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros** (Article 33 of the Law and Article 44 of the Regulations)[cite: 11].

The fund shall be set up using contributions from operators who, being obliged to set up a financial guarantee, take out insurance[cite: 11].

Contributions are collected by insurers alongside the commercial premiums applied to their policyholders for mandatory financial guarantee coverage[cite: 11]. In all cases, the beneficiaries and recipients of FCDM resources are exclusively the operators[cite: 11].

The Resolution of 31 October 2018 of the Directorate General for Insurance and Pension Funds approved the rate of contributions to the FCDM, setting it at 8% of the commercial premium amount applied by insurers to their policyholders for mandatory financial guarantee coverage[cite: 11]. This contribution applies to policies renewed or taken out from 30 October 2018 onward[cite: 11].

### The FCDM: purpose
**Environmental Damage Compensation Fund of the Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros** (Article 33 of the Law and Article 44 of the Regulations)[cite: 11].

The FCDM is intended to extend the coverage of insurance policies, under their same terms, for ceased activities that incurred environmental liability during the validity period of the policies, but which manifest or are claimed after the policy manifestation and claim periods (minimum 3 years)[cite: 11]. Furthermore, claims must be made within, at most, a number of years equal to the duration of the insurance policies, counted from the end of the last policy's validity and with a limit of 30 years; thus, the FCDM guarantee period is limited to 27 years for each operator[cite: 11].

The following are excluded from FCDM liability:

* Activities that did not have a valid insurance policy at the time activity ceased.
* Damage generated after activity ceased due to abandonment of facilities with potential polluting capacity.
* Damage that would not have been covered had the policy been valid[cite: 11].

FCDM coverage shall, at most, be the arithmetic mean of the sums insured in the last 5 annual insurance payments starting from the year in which the environmental damage occurred[cite: 11].

Pursuant to the Resolution of 31 October 2018 of the Presidency of the Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros approving the surcharge models in favor of the FCDM and establishing the declaration and deposit procedure, insurers must furnish policy conditions to the CCS for FCDM coverage purposes[cite: 11].

### The FCDM: current situation
The FCDM was created following the approval of Order APM/1040/2017 of 23 October, which established the dates for setting up mandatory financial guarantees[cite: 11].

The incorporation of insurance policies into the FCDM is being carried out progressively[cite: 11].

Thus, Appendix III activities classified as priority level 1 in Order ARM/1783/2011 must possess the mandatory financial guarantee starting 30 October 2018 and contribute to the FCDM upon the first issuance or renewal taking place after that date; priority level 2 activities join from 30 October 2019; and for priority level 3 activities, approval of the relevant ministerial order setting the mandatory date to contract a financial guarantee is still required[cite: 11].

In the coming years, it will be possible to run an assessment of the evolution of the FCDM, created as an environmental policy instrument linked to the insurance sector through insurer policies and managed and administered by the CCS[cite: 11].
